M&A Target Screening &
Valuation Intelligence

An acquisition-screening analytics platform that combines financial performance, valuation, growth, and sector context to rank and prioritize potential technology targets.

Focus
M&A Screening | Financial Analytics
Dataset
Public Market & Financial Data
01

Business Context

I wanted a consistent way to screen acquisition targets across valuation, growth, profitability, financial strength, cash generation, and returns. These measures did not always point in the same direction, a company could show strong growth while carrying a higher valuation or weaker financial profile. I treated the analysis as an early-stage screen rather than a substitute for deeper acquisition due diligence

The question: Which companies represent the strongest acquisition candidates when these financial factors are evaluated together?

I combined the measures into a scoring framework rather than ranking companies on a single metric, giving me a consistent basis for comparing targets and identifying a shortlist for further review.

02

Data & Workflow

I collected market and financial statement data for the selected technology companies through yfinance, then calculated the financial metrics needed to compare companies across valuation, growth, profitability, leverage, cash generation, and returns. I converted those measures into peer-relative scores and combined them into acquisition rankings, then stored the company and analytical data in SQLite for SQL analysis before exporting the final results to Power BI for screening and executive reporting.

03

Analysis & Dashboards

I used the scoring model as a starting point, then looked more closely at the financial and sector characteristics behind each company's acquisition ranking.

01

Growth vs. Valuation

I compared revenue growth with EV/Revenue to see which companies combined stronger growth with more reasonable valuations. I then viewed those positions alongside the acquisition scores to check whether the highest-ranked targets were also financially attractive on these two dimensions.

02

Financial Performance

I examined profitability, leverage, free cash flow, and return on equity to understand the financial quality behind each target. This helped separate companies that ranked well because of growth from those supported by stronger operating and balance-sheet characteristics.

03

Sector Intelligence

I compared acquisition scores across Enterprise Software and Cybersecurity to see whether attractiveness was concentrated in one part of the target universe. I also used the company-level view to make sure sector averages were not hiding stronger or weaker individual targets.

04

Key Findings

72.1 Top Acquisition Score
01

Top acquisition candidates separated from the broader universe

Adobe (72.1), Qualys (66.7), Intuit (65.2), and Microsoft (64.9) produced the four highest acquisition scores. Rather than one sector dominating the shortlist, the results included strong candidates from both Enterprise Software and Cybersecurity.

Multi-factor Acquisition Screen
02

Growth alone was not enough

Strong revenue growth did not consistently translate into a high acquisition score. Companies such as CrowdStrike and SentinelOne ranked lower once valuation and financial strength were considered alongside growth, reinforcing the need for a multi-factor screen.

57.7 Enterprise Software Avg.
03

Enterprise Software led at the sector level

Enterprise Software recorded an average acquisition score of 57.7, compared with 47.3 for Cybersecurity. Within the selected company universe, that gave Enterprise Software the stronger overall quantitative profile.

Qualys Cybersecurity Standout
04

Sector averages hid strong individual candidates

The lower Cybersecurity average did not tell the whole story: Qualys and Check Point still ranked among the stronger acquisition candidates. That made company-level screening important even when one sector appeared weaker overall.

05

Recommendations

I'd extend the analysis beyond the current Enterprise Software and Cybersecurity groups before drawing broader conclusions about technology acquisition opportunities. A larger peer universe would also provide a stronger basis for the percentile rankings used in the scoring model.

The quantitative screen is useful for narrowing candidates, but I would treat it as the starting point for deeper diligence. The next stage should incorporate factors such as strategic fit, management quality, competitive position, and integration considerations before any target moves forward.

Because the final acquisition score depends on the weights assigned to valuation, growth, profitability, financial strength, cash generation, and returns, I'd test alternative weighting scenarios. That would show which candidates remain strong across different priorities and which rankings depend heavily on the current assumptions.

06

Scope & Limitations

I built this as an early-stage screening framework, so the acquisition scores support prioritization rather than transaction valuation or an acquisition recommendation. The model is based on financial and market measures, which means factors outside the available quantitative data are not reflected in the rankings. The scores are also relative to the selected technology-company peer group, so they should be interpreted within that defined universe rather than as absolute measures of acquisition attractiveness.
