Macy's Store Portfolio Strategy

A public-data decision-support case study examining Macy's store portfolio evolution and the financial context behind portfolio decisions.

Focus
Retail Portfolio Strategy
Data
Store Disclosures | SEC Filings
01

Business Context

Macy's is shrinking its store footprint while directing more investment toward selected go-forward locations. What made this interesting to me was that the public data shows where the portfolio is changing, but not whether an individual store is underperforming, Macy's doesn't disclose store-level profitability, traffic, or comparable sales. That gap limited how far I could take the analysis with public data alone.

The question: How can Macy's use portfolio and financial evidence to make more selective decisions about store rationalization, intervention, and reinvestment?

Rather than trying to predict which specific store closes next, I used store history, announced closures, company financials, and Macy's reported strategy to consider when Invest, Transform, Monitor, or Exit might be the more defensible call.

02

Data & Workflow

I started by bringing Macy's quarterly store disclosures into a consistent location history, then tracked which locations persisted or disappeared and reconciled those changes against the announced closure records. Once I knew what was happening to the footprint, I added annual and quarterly financial results and Macy's reported strategy to understand the business context, then brought the combined evidence into Power BI for the Invest, Transform, Monitor, or Exit framework.

03

Analysis & Dashboards

I used the portfolio history to understand how Macy's footprint was changing, then connected those movements with announced closures, financial performance, and the company's reported strategy.

01

Portfolio Evolution

I built this view to follow locations from one reporting period to the next rather than relying on a simple first-versus-last comparison. Looking at the timing and regional distribution of those movements helped me separate gradual portfolio change from periods where the footprint shifted more sharply.

02

Closure Analysis

I treated the announced closure list as a separate source and reconciled it against the location history I had already built. That gave me a way to check how closely Macy's disclosed closures aligned with observed portfolio changes without using one dataset as a substitute for the other.

03

Recommendation Framework

I brought the portfolio analysis together with Macy's financial results and reported Reimagine strategy because contraction alone didn't answer what should happen next. The final view turns that evidence into four possible actions — Invest, Transform, Monitor, or Exit — while leaving individual store decisions dependent on internal performance data.

04

Key Findings

16.4% Footprint Reduction
01

Macy's footprint contracted by 16.4%

Across the 11 reporting periods, the portfolio moved from 488 location IDs to 408. The decline was substantial enough that I wanted to understand when those locations disappeared rather than treating the change as one continuous trend.

54 Locations Disappeared
02

The contraction was concentrated in late 2024

The biggest shift came between Q3 and Q4 2024, when 54 locations disappeared from the reported portfolio. That concentration showed that much of the restructuring happened in a distinct wave rather than gradually across the full period.

92.9% Closure Match Rate
03

13 of 14 announced closures matched the historical portfolio

I was able to reconcile 13 announced closures to historical locations, a 92.9% match rate, while one remained unresolved. I kept those confirmed records separate from other portfolio disappearances instead of assuming every missing location represented a closure.

Reimagine 125 Go-Forward Locations
04

Reinvestment was part of the story, not just contraction

Macy's reported stronger FY2025 performance from its Reimagine 125 locations. That mattered for my recommendation because it provided evidence that selected locations may warrant intervention or reinvestment before exit.

05

Recommendations

Based on the mix of portfolio disappearances and confirmed closures, I'd evaluate locations individually rather than treating footprint reduction as the answer by default. The next action could be Invest, Transform, Monitor, or Exit, depending on the evidence for that location.

Macy's reported performance from Reimagine 125 gives a reason to test targeted improvements before exiting every underperforming location. I'd reserve closure for cases where continued weakness and strategic relevance no longer justify further investment.

The public data can show where the portfolio changed, but it cannot determine the right action for an individual store. I'd add sales, margins, traffic, lease costs, and customer measures before using this framework for actual location-level decisions.

06

Scope & Limitations

I worked only with publicly available data, so the analysis can describe Macy's portfolio changes but cannot measure the underlying performance of individual stores. The closure disclosure also covers only announced closures, which means it cannot explain every change observed across the broader store history. Without store-level sales, profitability, traffic, lease costs, and comparable sales, I couldn't assign individual locations to Invest, Transform, Monitor, or Exit; with access to those measures, I'd use the framework as the starting point for actual store-level decisions.
